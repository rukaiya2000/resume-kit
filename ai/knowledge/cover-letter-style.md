# Cover letter style

A cover letter is a short argument for **this** job, built from the same facts as the tailored resume. It never introduces new claims.

## Shape (3–5 paragraphs, under 350 words)

1. **Hook, 2–3 sentences:** the role, and the single strongest reason you fit, tied to something specific in the job description (the product, the problem, the team's stack). No “I am writing to apply for…”.
2. **Proof, 1–2 paragraphs:** the two or three requirements that matter most, each backed by a concrete result from the resume or notes, with numbers where the source has them. Show how you did it, not just what.
3. **Why them, optional:** one short paragraph connecting your side projects or interests (e.g. AI agents, evals) to what the company does. Only if the job description or company gives you something real to connect to.
4. **Close, 1–2 sentences:** what you'd bring in the first months and a simple call to talk. No begging, no “I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience”.

## Voice

- First person, confident, plain. Short sentences beat long ones.
- Mirror the job description's words for the same things (“platform reliability”, “LLM evaluation”).
- Every number must already exist in a fact source, written the same way (“cut API response times by 90%”).
- Don't repeat the resume bullet by bullet; explain the one or two stories behind it.
- No clichés: “passionate”, “team player”, “hit the ground running”, “fast-paced environment”, “synergy”.
- A real gap is never papered over. If it matters, name adjacent evidence honestly (“I haven't run Kubernetes in production, but I containerized and deployed…”) only when that evidence exists.

## Greeting and closing

- Use the hiring manager's name if the job note or description gives it; otherwise “Dear Hiring Manager,”.
- Close with “Sincerely,” (or “Best regards,”). The signature is the name from the resume header.
